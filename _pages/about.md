I am Thu Hang Ha, an early-career researcher from Vietnam with an interdisciplinary background in energy economics, sustainability analysis, and data-driven decision-making. My academic training bridges economic reasoning with quantitative modeling to understand how complex energy and industrial systems can be designed, evaluated, and improved in practice.

My research interests lie at the intersection of energy systems, environmental sustainability, and industrial operations, with a particular focus on how analytical and modeling approaches can support evidence-based decisions at both policy and operational levels. I am especially interested in sustainability challenges faced by emerging economies, where rapid economic growth, energy demand, and climate commitments must be addressed simultaneously.

Research Orientation
======
My work combines system-level analysis with applied, data-driven methods to examine sustainability transitions in energy and industrial sectors. I am motivated by questions such as how low-carbon technologies can be evaluated under real-world constraints, how industrial systems can be optimized to reduce emissions, and how quantitative evidence can inform long-term planning and policy design.

While my academic foundation is rooted in modeling and analysis, I view research as a means to generate practical and policy-relevant insights, rather than an end in itself.

What I Work On
======
My academic and applied interests currently focus on:

- **Energy systems and sustainability modeling**, including emissions assessment and scenario analysis.

- **Techno-economic evaluation** of renewable energy and low-carbon solutions for industrial applications.

- **Data-driven approaches** to support decision-making in energy, environment, and industrial systems.

These themes are reflected across my publications and applied projects.


From Research to Impact
------
I am particularly interested in research that connects theoretical frameworks with real-world implementation, especially in the context of Vietnam and other developing or transitioning economies. In the long term, I aspire to contribute to sustainable development by translating rigorous academic training into tangible impact—whether through research, policy-oriented work, or collaboration with industry and public institutions.
